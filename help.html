KITCHEN · HOW TO

Using the Kitchen app

Plan the fortnight, shop for it, cook from the recipe, and keep a diary of what you ate. Buttons are shown LIKE THIS, exactly as they read in the app.

1. A week with the app

  1. Find ideas. Browse RECIPES, and tap the + on any card (in the Modernist style a little list-with-plus picture) to put it on the shortlist.
  2. Plan. In PLANNER, tap a shortlisted recipe, then tap the day you'll cook it.
  3. Shop. SHOPPING adds up everything the week needs, sorted by aisle. Tick things off as you go.
  4. Remind. If Google Calendar is connected, the days go into your calendar, with a reminder the evening before (handy for the freezer).
  5. Cook. Open the recipe on the tablet, switch on COOKING MODE, and tick the boxes as you go.
  6. Remember. Ticking the last column logs the meal in HISTORY, and asks which meal it was.

2. Finding recipes

Everything starts from RECIPES in the sidebar.

  • Search by name, ingredient or note. Press Enter to keep what you typed as a tag while you search for something else.
  • Filters: COURSE (tap several), SOURCE, and KEYWORD (tap a faint suggestion, or + ADD your own). CLEAR ALL FILTERS starts again. In MODERNIST the filters are behind a button, as Two looks says.
  • Sort by RECENTLY ADDED, LEAST COOKED, MOST COOKED, RECENTLY COOKED or A – Z.
  • Saved views in the sidebar are one-tap filters, for example Weeknight, under 30 min or Not cooked in 3 months. Tap again to turn one off.
  • On each card: + (a little list-with-plus picture in the Modernist style) puts it on the shortlist (it turns to ✓), and ☆ makes it a favourite. Tap anywhere else to open it.
  • A time with EST. beside it was worked out by the converter from the method, because the source gave no overall time. It is a good guess, not the source's figure, and it counts in Weeknight, under 30 min like any other. If you know better, EDIT the recipe and type the real time into TIME: the EST. goes with the old figure.

3. Cooking from a recipe

Reading the diagram

Ingredients are on the left. Each column is a moment in time, read left to right: boxes join together as things are combined, with a time on each step (8 MIN, OVERNIGHT, ~ UNTIL DONE). A step that looks after itself once it is going (simmering, baking, resting, chilling) says so after its time, 25 MIN · HANDS OFF, and its bar in the timeline is hatched instead of solid. A step with no such word is one you are busy with, as it always was. Overnight steps are hands off by themselves.

Ticking as you go

  • Tap any box to tick it off. Ticking a step also ticks everything that went into it.
  • Ticks last until you reload. RESET TICKS clears them.
  • The timeline bar above the diagram follows your ticks. A step you tick turns grey with a ✓ on its bar, and goes back to colour if you untick it or press RESET TICKS. Tapping a box also flashes its bar, when the bar is on the screen. Tapping a bar does the reverse: it takes you to that step's box. A step that takes no time (INSTANT) is a small diamond with its name beside it, and every other bar is wide enough to tap, however short the step.
  • Ticking the whole last column logs the recipe as cooked today. A box asks Which meal was that? (Breakfast, Lunch, Dinner, Snack), with room for a note on how it went. SKIP keeps the log without a meal.

Cooking for more or fewer people

Under the header, COOK FOR rescales the amounts for this time only: tap 2, 4, 6 or 8, or type a number and GO. RESET goes back to the recipe as written. Nothing is saved, and tin sizes are never scaled. A liquid changes unit as it grows or shrinks: under 15 ml it reads in teaspoons, under 50 ml in tablespoons and from 50 ml in ml, so 3 tbsp of oil for twice the people reads 90 ml. Amounts in grams, such as honey weighed out, stay in grams. A solid the converter turned from spoons into grams keeps its spoons in brackets, and they change with it: 30 g light brown sugar (2 tbsp) for twice the people reads 60 g light brown sugar (4 tbsp).

Keeping the screen on

COOKING MODE stops the screen going to sleep, hides the buttons you don't need at the hob, and tucks the sidebar away (☰ brings it back). It was called KEEP AWAKE until October 2026. Scale the recipe before you switch it on. SHOW TIMELINE adds the overview bar back if you want it.

When to chop: preparation as steps

A recipe line such as "2 leeks, thinly sliced" is read, as the recipe is written, as done before you start. To have it as a step of its own instead, Thinly slice the leeks, beside the step before the one that needs it, choose AS STEPS IN THE FLOW under PREPARATION in Settings → APP. BEFORE YOU START (AS WRITTEN) puts it back. It is for this device only, so the tablet at the hob and the laptop can differ. Nothing in the recipe, the timeline or the shopping list changes; a step the app can't read (an unusual word) simply stays as written.

When to start: EAT AT, your hands and the ovens

The timeline over a recipe, or over a group, is also a run sheet. EAT AT is when you want to eat. It starts as now plus the longest dish, up to the next five minutes, so you can start now and eat when it is ready. Change it with −15, −5, +5 and +15; NOW brings back the first time. The ruler then reads times of day, every box says AT 17:20 when to start it, and START AT says when the meal begins. EAT AT belongs to what is open: nothing is saved, and it starts again from the default when you open something else, or reload.

The row YOUR HANDS shows when a step needs you: solid where you are busy, red and hatched where two steps want you at once (a clash), and an outline such as free 16 min where nothing needs you for six minutes or more. A step marked HANDS OFF never needs you, and a recipe with no step marked yet is left out of this row (a group names the dishes left out), because without a mark every step looks as if it needs you. AVOID THE CLASH appears only when there is a clash. It moves the later step, and what it needs, earlier until nothing overlaps, so that dish is ready a little before you serve. Nothing moves until you press it. Once pressed it turns into a filled ✓ CLASH AVOIDED · TAP TO UNDO, and tapping that puts everything back.

A sentence under the heading says what the group is doing: with FINISH TOGETHER every dish is timed to be ready at your EAT AT time and the shorter ones start later; with START TOGETHER every dish starts at once and the shorter ones are ready earlier. Each dish says READY AT as well as START AT. When the start time has already gone for a meal planned for today, THAT TIME HAS PASSED: TAP NOW appears; NOW brings back the first time.

If a recipe says Preheat the oven to 195°C (fan) on its steps, the timeline shows a thin lane for each oven with the setting and the dish, and says when a meal wants more oven settings at once than your kitchen has. Two dishes at the same setting share one oven. Tell the app how many ovens you have under OVENS in Settings → APP (for this device only; a device you have not set has one). A recipe it cannot read an oven temperature from (none, a range, or two) is left out of that check, and the strip says so.

START COOKING starts the run at the real time, and EAT AT stops following the clock. From then, tick a step as you finish it: the plan is drawn again from the time you ticked. A step ticked late is drawn as long as it took and what comes after it moves later; a step ticked early is not pulled forward. A line says On time for 18:30 or Ready at 18:43, 13 min after 18:30, and a Now line says what is going, how long it has left, and when your hands are next wanted (Hands free for 9 min, then: …). A red line down the strip is the real time, and a faint dotted mark shows where a step had been planned. A step already ticked when you press START COOKING counts as done then. If two steps would want your hands at once, the later one waits, so the meal reads late; AVOID THE CLASH is still there to move it earlier instead. STOP COOKING puts the plan back, and RESET TICKS clears the times along with the ticks. Nothing is saved: a reload ends the run, as it ends the ticks.

Also on the recipe page

  • ⇄ SUBSTITUTION RECOMMENDATIONS lists your swaps that apply, with amounts worked out.
  • COOKING NOTES: jot how it went with ADD A NOTE. Each note is dated. FOLD INTO NOTES moves one into the recipe's own notes when it's worth keeping for good.
  • Source chips under the title say whether the ingredients have been checked against the original, and SOURCE PHOTO opens the photo or PDF it came from.
  • PRINT / A4 PDF and EXPORT PNG for a paper copy or a picture to send.
  • + SHORTLIST, EDIT and DELETE. Deleting a recipe also deletes its cooking history and notes, and can't be undone.

4. Cooking several dishes together

When a day has two or more recipes (a main and a side, say), GROUP SOME TOGETHER on the Planner lets you tick the ones you're cooking at once, then GROUP SELECTED. Tap the GROUPED: … badge to see them together: one timeline with a lane for each dish, each headed by the dish's name and colour, and each recipe's diagram below. Ticking greys a dish's own bars only. UNGROUP separates them again; they stay on the day.

Above the timeline, START TOGETHER starts every dish at once, and FINISH TOGETHER lines them up to be ready at the same moment: the longest dish reads START FIRST, and each shorter one says when to begin it, as a time of day, for example START AT 17:35. The timeline's last mark is the time you eat (EAT AT, below). A group with a dish whose course is Side opens in FINISH TOGETHER; any other group opens in START TOGETHER. The choice lasts until you leave the app and changes nothing saved. When the longest dish has a step with no time in brackets, the start times say ABOUT, because that step is counted as two minutes. While cooking, the timeline and its switch are behind SHOW TIMELINE; the start times stay on each dish.

When a recipe says to serve it with something (serve with rice or naan, or a line such as naan, to serve) and your library has a recipe whose course is Side with that word in its title or keywords, the recipe shows SERVE WITH: and the side's name under its chips. Tap the name to open the side. Up to three are shown, the one you cook most first. A good side has course=Side, the staple's name in its title or a keyword (a side called Jewelled pot tagged Rice works), and a time in brackets on every step, without which it has no lane in a group's timeline. Garnishes such as lime wedges, to serve show nothing unless you have a side of that name.

On the PLANNER, the same sides are offered under the dish: SERVE WITH: and a + button for each. Tap one and the side goes on that day, for as many people as the dish, and is grouped with it, so the two open together with the side timed to finish with the dish. Once a side for it is on the day, the offer goes. × hides the offer for that dish on that day until you next open the app; nothing is saved. Take the side off again with its own ×: as with any dish, its group goes too.

OFTEN WITH: works the same way from your own groups: the recipes you have grouped with a dish before (once is enough), whatever their course, the one grouped most often first, two at most. It shows in the recipe and under the dish on the PLANNER, where + adds one to the day and groups it with the dish. A recipe already offered under SERVE WITH is not repeated, and one already on the day is not offered. A group you UNGROUP, or whose dish you take off the day, is gone, so it no longer counts.

5. Planning the fortnight

PLANNER shows the next fourteen days, rolling forward each day, grouped into weeks.

  • CHOOSE A RECIPE opens the library: tap a recipe to put it on that day. ADD ANOTHER adds a second dish.
  • MARK DAY AWAY (while choosing) marks a day as deliberately blank, such as eating out or away.
  • OTHER (the one with a +) adds something that isn't a recipe to that day, such as a takeaway, a meal out or leftovers: what it is and, if you like, the meal. It shows under the day's dishes, tagged AD HOC, with a × to take it off (a day that hasn't happened yet asks nothing; today or earlier asks first). Tap its tag, AD HOC or for example DINNER · AD HOC, to set the meal, change it, or take it off. It is the same entry + ENTRY makes on History, so it is in the food diary on that day. It isn't on the shopping list or in the Google Calendar.
  • The SERVES button sets how many people a dish is for on that day. The shopping list follows it, and so does the recipe when you open it from the Planner: it opens already worked out for that number, and RESET on the card puts it back to the amounts as the recipe is written.
  • × takes a dish off the day. A bar in that day says what went, with UNDO for ten seconds: tap it and the dish comes back in its place, with its number of people and the group it was in. The bar goes by itself, and if you leave the Planner or change the day in the meantime.

From the freezer, and cooked

  • Every dish on the Planner starts as TO COOK. Tap it if the dish is already cooked and coming out of the freezer. It asks which meal it's for (breakfast, lunch, dinner or snack) and then reads, for example, PRE-MADE · DINNER. The dish leaves the shopping list, and on its day it goes into the food diary by itself, marked from the freezer. Tap it again to make it fresh. Switched back on as a different meal, the food diary changes to match.
  • COOKED ✓ appears on today's dishes. Tap it when you've cooked one without ticking through its recipe: it's logged just as finishing the recipe would, and asks which meal it was. Afterwards it reads ✓ COOKED.
  • A meal from the freezer is eaten, not cooked: it doesn't change "last cooked", times cooked or the streak.
  • Finish the recipe of a dish marked PRE-MADE for today and it asks Cooked fresh, or from the freezer? FROM THE FREEZER leaves the diary as it is. COOKED FRESH turns PRE-MADE off for that dish (it goes back to TO COOK), swaps its freezer entry for a cooked one, and asks which meal it was.

The shortlist

Ideas waiting for a day. Add recipes with + on a card, or type a note in the box (for something you haven't got a recipe for yet). To plan one: tap the shortlisted recipe, then tap a day. It moves onto that day. A note goes the same way: tap it, then tap a day, and it becomes an entry on that day, the same kind OTHER makes (tap its tag if you want to give it a meal). CANCEL or Esc stops.

6. Your Google Calendar

Planned days can go into a Google Calendar of their own, called RecipeWrangler, as an all-day event listing that day's dishes. Connect it once in Settings → APP → CALENDAR with CONNECT GOOGLE CALENDAR. Google says it hasn't verified the app: choose Advanced, then Go to RecipeWrangler, and allow.

On the Planner

  • IN CALENDAR puts that day in the calendar. Tap again to take it out.
  • REMIND ME … THE EVENING BEFORE adds a phone reminder the night before, for getting things out of the freezer.
  • A day with more than one meal has a REMIND ME 20:00 on each meal instead, so a day can remind you about the dinner and not the breakfast. Dishes you put together with GROUP SOME TOGETHER are one meal and have one reminder, on their GROUPED bar. A dish you add to a day that is in the calendar goes in without a reminder. In the calendar such a day is two events, one with the reminder and one without. Putting dishes with different reminders into one group gives them all the reminder.
  • ADD THE WEEK, by a week's heading, puts the whole week in at once.
  • Changing a day's dishes updates its event; marking it away or emptying it removes the event.

In Settings

  • AUTOMATICALLY ADD TO CALENDAR: every day you plan goes in by itself.
  • AUTOMATICALLY REMIND ME FOR MAIN MEALS: a meal with a Main-course recipe in it, or a dish coming out of the freezer, gets the evening-before reminder. The day's other meals go in the calendar without one.
  • Switching either on also catches up the days you've already planned. Switching off changes nothing already in the calendar.
  • REMIND AT sets the reminder time (20:00 to start with). SYNC NOW brings everything into step.
  • DISCONNECT stops the app updating the calendar. The calendar and its events stay in Google.

7. Shopping

SHOPPING adds up every ingredient the week's planned dishes need, scaled to each day's servings and sorted into aisles. A line that says frozen ("200 g frozen peas") is listed under FROZEN, at the end of the shop; anything else can be moved there in Settings → SHOPPING AISLES, or from the list itself in AUDIT.

  • Choose this week, next week or BOTH WEEKS from the list at the top. Both weeks keeps its own ticks.
  • To shop for one recipe on its own, open it and press SHOPPING LIST. It lists that recipe's ingredients by aisle, for the number of people COOK FOR is showing. Its ticks are kept on this device until you leave the page and never touch the week's list. BACK TO WEEK returns to the week and OPEN RECIPE goes back to the recipe; the sidebar's SHOPPING always shows the week.
  • Spoons and ml of the same liquid are added together. From 50 ml the total is in ml, with the recipes' own amounts after it, e.g. Olive oil — 145 ml (100 ml + 3 tbsp); under 50 ml it stays in spoons.
  • Tick items as they go in the trolley. Ticks show on every device, so two people can shop from the same list.
  • HIDE TICKED tidies the list; CLEAR TICKS unticks everything.
  • AUDIT at the top is for fixing the list, not shopping from it. While it is on, each row shows MERGE WITH…, to join it to another name for good, and its aisle with why it is there: pick another and the row moves (UNDO takes it back). If the list thinks two names are the same thing it asks Same as …?: MERGE adds them together from now on, KEEP APART stops it asking. Off, the button says how many questions are waiting, e.g. AUDIT · 2. END AUDIT hides it all again; each device remembers which. Merging two rows when only one is ticked unticks the merged row, so check you have enough. Moving an aisle never loses a tick.
  • ⇄ POSSIBLE SWAPS at the bottom lists alternatives you've saved.
  • PRINT / A4 PDF or EXPORT PNG to take it with you. Ticked means you already have it, so ticked items are left off both, and so is an aisle with nothing left to buy.

8. History and the food diary

HISTORY is a month calendar of what you've eaten. Tap a day to see it; tap a dish to open its recipe.

  • Cooked recipes arrive here when you tick the last column of their diagram, or tap COOKED ✓ on the Planner. Meals marked PRE-MADE on the Planner arrive by themselves on their day, marked FROM THE FREEZER.
  • + ENTRY logs something with no recipe card, such as a takeaway or a meal out: what it was, the date and, if you like, the meal. The OTHER button on a day of the Planner makes the same entry for that day.
  • REMOVE takes an entry out. For a meal from the freezer it also takes PRE-MADE off that dish on the Planner, or it would come straight back.
  • CSV downloads the whole diary for a spreadsheet.
  • The STATS panel shows totals, your current streak and what you cook most.

9. Adding a recipe

NEW RECIPE at the foot of the sidebar opens the form.

  1. Paste a converted recipe into the box (it's read straight away), or use WRITE ONE BY HAND for a starting template. PARSE & PREVIEW reads it again after changes.
  2. Look over WHAT THE APP READ and the checks underneath. They're advice only; nothing stops you saving.
  3. For a recipe from a website, COMPARE WITH SOURCE (it often runs by itself) lists the page's ingredients beside yours. An ingredient written on several lines on either side (3 tbsp of oil on the page, 1 tbsp then 2 tbsp in yours) is shown once, with every line and what they add up to. The table is sorted under headings with a count each (missing, extra, more, less, a different word), the biggest difference first, each saying how far apart it is; what matches is folded away at the bottom until you tap SHOW. If your pasted list repeats a measured line, it says so: some pages print each ingredient again under the step that uses it. If the site won't allow it, OPEN SOURCE ↗, copy its ingredient list, paste it in and COMPARE PASTED LIST. When it's right, VALIDATE INGREDIENT LIST.
  4. From a book or a card? ADD PHOTO OR PDF keeps the page with the recipe (each one is numbered FILE 1, FILE 2…; a PDF of several pages is one file, kept whole), and CHECKED AGAINST THE PHOTO records that you've checked it.
  5. Want a picture of your own on the card? UPLOAD PHOTO beside IMAGE URL takes one from the tablet or phone instead of a web link; it is stored with the recipe when you save.
  6. Fill in the source and servings (both needed), then SAVE RECIPE.

SCALE TO SERVE in the form changes the recipe for good. To cook a different amount just once, use COOK FOR on the recipe page instead.

Adding several recipes at once

Converted a whole batch? ADD SEVERAL, under NEW RECIPE, takes them all in one go. It only adds new recipes: it never changes one you already have.

  1. Have the recipes in one file, or one after another in a single message, as the converter gives them. Each one starts with a line beginning TITLE:. A code block round each one works, and so does none.
  2. CHOOSE A FILE, or paste them all into the box (they are read as soon as you paste; READ THEM reads them again).
  3. Look down the list. A recipe is ticked unless it is held back (it has no source, no servings or nothing to cook; the list says which, and where in your text) or looks like one you already have (the same source link or title; it is shown and left unticked, and nothing is merged). SHOW THE RECIPE opens one to look at it. Untick any you don't want.
  4. Under SOURCES, if a source is spelt differently from one you already have, answer as in the form: USE THAT or KEEP MINE.
  5. The button at the foot counts what is ticked. Press it. The recipes appear at once and save in the background.
  6. Then check each one. OPEN beside a recipe in the list takes you into it, ready for COMPARE WITH SOURCE. A batch is not compared with its pages for you: Settings → Library → LIBRARY CHECK lists the recipes not yet compared.

Added something by mistake? Delete that recipe the usual way; there is no single undo for a whole batch.

Converting your own recipes

The app reads recipes written in its own layout (groups of ingredients, then stages). Turning a web page, a photo of a book or a card into that layout is done in the Claude app, by a project that follows the app's conversion instructions. A household new to the app sets one up once:

  1. Download the instructions: conversion-instructions.md on GitHub (the download button is at the top right of the file), or the plain file to save from the browser.
  2. In the Claude app, make a new project, for example "Recipe conversion", and add that file to the project's files. That one file is all it needs.
  3. For each recipe, start a chat in that project and give it the web address, a photo of the page, or the recipe's text. It works through the recipe and replies with the recipe in the app's layout, then only what it needs you to check or answer. A photo or PDF you gave it comes back renamed to the recipe's title, ready to attach as the recipe's source; several recipes in one message come back one by one.
  4. Copy its reply, then NEW RECIPE here and paste it in, as above.

The instructions are improved from time to time; the link above is always the latest. When they change, the app says so in a CONVERTER bar: download the file again and replace the one in the project. A recipe another household shares needs no converting: ADD TO OUR RECIPES copies it as it is (section 11).

10. Settings

  • INGREDIENTS: look up how the shopping list names an ingredient and which aisle it goes in; teach it that two words are the same (WORD MATCHES); move an ingredient to another aisle (SHOPPING AISLES); and keep your SWAPS. Each list folds: tap its name, which shows how many it holds, to fold or open it, and this device remembers. Typing in INGREDIENT LOOKUP narrows every list at once and opens the ones with a match; clear it and they go back as you left them.
  • LIBRARY: LIBRARY CHECK lists recipes still to be checked against their source, and under a recipe that writes one ingredient on several lines (a splash of soy sauce, then more later) says so, with the two-different-units ones first, because a comparison with its page is what shows whether they add up (the box beside it shows only those); tidy SOURCES and KEYWORDS; look after recipe photos.
  • APP: which day weeks start on, light or dark (APPEARANCE), how the app looks (STYLE), Google Calendar, and RECIPE SHARING. Changing the day weeks start on asks first if anything on the shopping list is ticked, because the ticks belong to the old weeks and are cleared.
  • HELP: this guide, and the app's version with what changed lately.

Two looks. STYLE chooses between CLASSIC, the paper and plum look, and MODERNIST, which is flat and square, in one typeface, with red for the main action and for what is switched on. Everything works the same in both, light or dark, and the choice is for this device only, so the tablet and a phone can differ. Four things behave differently in MODERNIST:

  • On RECIPES the filters are behind a FILTERS button beside the search, to leave the recipe cards the room. Tap it and a panel slides in from the right with COURSE, SOURCE, SORT and every KEYWORD, the ones that are on first and then the most used. The box above the keywords narrows them as you type (any part of a word will do); if nothing matches it says so, and Enter adds what you typed as a keyword filter. + ADD takes you to that box. Each tap changes the recipes behind it at once. The red button at the foot, which says how many recipes are left, closes the panel; so do the × at its top, a tap outside it, and Esc. CLEAR switches every filter off. What is on shows under the search as small chips, with a number on FILTERS: tap a chip to switch that one off, or CLEAR ALL to switch off the lot. If there are more chips than fit on the line, a +3 or so at the end opens the panel.
  • On the Planner, on a recipe in cooking mode, and on every screen when the window is narrower than 1100 pixels (the tablet held upright), the side menu folds to a narrow strip down the left. Tap the strip to open the menu; the ‹ at its top folds it again, and so does choosing a screen.
  • When a recipe's table of steps is wider than the screen, a small square with an arrow (plum in Classic, dark in Modernist) sits at its top right corner. It stays there while you scroll the table sideways, and goes when you reach the end. It is left out of EXPORT PNG and of printing.
  • The Planner's shortlist folds to a strip down the right with its › button, to give the days more room. Tap the strip, or SHORTLIST in the side menu, to open it again. It opens again by itself next time you start the app.
  • On a recipe card, the ✓ that shows a recipe is on the shortlist takes it off again when tapped.

11. Sharing recipes with family

Another household of the family can have the app too, with its own recipes, plan and diary. Either household can let the other browse its recipes and copy the ones it wants. Only recipes are shared: never the plan, the diary, cooking notes, the shopping list or the calendar.

Letting them browse ours

  1. Settings → APP → RECIPE SHARING. Give our household a name the other side will recognise, and tap SAVE.
  2. Type the email someone in their household signs in with, and tap SHARE. They appear under SHARED WITH.
  3. To stop, tap STOP SHARING beside them. Anything they have already copied stays theirs.

Each side switches it on for itself: they share theirs with us in the same way.

Browsing theirs

  • Once a household shares with us, the top of RECIPES has a switch: OUR RECIPES and theirs, by name. Search and the filters work on theirs just as on ours.
  • Their recipe opens read-only. Read it, tick through it, use COOK FOR, print it; finishing it logs nothing, because it isn't in our library.
  • ADD TO OUR RECIPES makes our own copy, photo and all, and opens it. From then on it is ours, to plan, shop for, edit and log; changes they make to theirs don't reach it.
  • A card of theirs that we already have says ALREADY IN OUR RECIPES, and the button reads OPEN OUR COPY.

12. When something goes wrong

The OFFLINE bar

No connection. Carry on: changes are kept on the device and sent when you're back, and you'll see Back online — sending your changes.

Couldn't save …

A change didn't reach the server. It's kept and tried again with your next change. If it keeps happening, check the connection.

The UPDATE bar

"A new version is ready": the app was changed while this tab was open. "Part-way through an update": it arrived in pieces. Either way, tap RELOAD. What changed is under VERSION AND WHAT'S NEW in Settings → HELP.

The CONVERTER bar

"The recipe converter's instructions have changed": a change to the app also changed the instructions your household's conversion project follows (section 9). Nothing in the app stops working, and you can carry on under it. When the change is a new rule for the converter to follow, such as marking the steps that look after themselves, reload the app on every device first, then do this, so that a recipe converted with the new rule reads correctly wherever you open it. When you have a minute:

  1. Tap OPEN THE INSTRUCTIONS ↗ and download the file.
  2. In the Claude app, open your household's conversion project and replace its copy of the file with the new one.
  3. Back here, tap DONE. That covers every device in your household, and the bar won't come back until the instructions change again.

LATER hides it until the app is next opened.

Another device looks out of date

Switch to another app and back, or reload. The app picks up other devices' changes when you return to it.

A photo won't show

It needs a connection, and a photo added on another device shows once its upload has finished.

The calendar says there's a problem

Look in Settings → APP → CALENDAR. Try SYNC NOW; if it says Google no longer accepts the connection, CONNECT GOOGLE CALENDAR again. Nothing in your plan is lost.

13. Backups and admin

  • EXPORT DATA (foot of the sidebar) downloads a backup of everything. Keep one before any big change.
  • IMPORT DATA restores a backup. It replaces everything, on every device, so use it with care.
  • Settings → APP → BACKUP TO GOOGLE DRIVE: once Google is connected, the app saves the same file as EXPORT DATA into a folder called RecipeWrangler backups in your Google Drive, once a week by itself, keeping the last 8. SAVE TO DRIVE NOW saves one straight away. To restore one, download it from Drive and use IMPORT DATA. If Google was connected before this existed, tap CONNECT AGAIN FOR DRIVE once. The backups go to the Google account of whoever in the household connected Google, and they hold the whole household's recipes, plans and diary.
  • SIGN OUT is at the foot of the sidebar too.
  • Settings → LIBRARY → RECIPE PHOTOS: RE-HOST EXTERNAL IMAGES copies web photos into your own storage, and CHECK STORED IMAGES checks they're all intact.

The recipe text, in brief

Recipes are plain text. Header lines first, then groups of ingredients that go in together, then stages: each stage is one column of the diagram, and MERGE joins groups into the next box, with a time in square brackets. A step that looks after itself adds , hands off inside the brackets: [25 min, hands off]. It goes after a time or until …, never after [instant] and never on its own.

TITLE: My recipe
SOURCE: Personal recipe
SERVINGS: 4
TAGS: course=Main

GROUP base:
2 onions, sliced
1 tbsp rapeseed oil

GROUP finish:
1 lemon, juiced

STAGE:
MERGE base -> cooked: soften the onions [10 min]

STAGE:
MERGE cooked, finish -> dish: stir in the lemon and serve [instant]

Groups that are merged must sit next to each other, and anything added at the end gets a group of its own. NOTES:, TIPS:, STORING: and FREEZING: sections can follow.
